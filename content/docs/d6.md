**Evidence cutoff:** 19 November 2026 · **Use:** obligations and evidence for an APRA-regulated entity with telecom and payment dependencies

## Regulatory boundary

**Fact.** CPS 230 has applied since 1 July 2025, with targeted amendments from 1 July 2026 ([APRA current CPS 230](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230); [amendment notice](https://www.apra.gov.au/consultations/operational-risk-management)). Its subject is operational risk, critical-operation continuity and material service providers. [CPS 220, paras 21–29](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-220) establishes the broader board-approved risk framework and appetite. [CPS 190](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-190) addresses recovery and orderly exit when financial viability is threatened; CPS 230 para. 18 requires continuity planning to be consistent with it. These duties interact but answer different questions. Applicability to any particular bank, carrier, payment or cloud asset must be checked against the legal entity and asset definitions.

**Regime: CPS 230** **Responsible party and operative duty:** APRA-regulated entity: identify critical operations; set board-approved maximum disruption, maximum data loss and minimum alternative service tolerances; map people, technology, information, facilities and providers; test severe but plausible disruptions. Payments, deposit-taking/management, custody, settlements and clearing are ADI minimum critical operations unless justified otherwise; customer enquiries and supporting infrastructure are cross-industry minima. **Evidence a board or operator should retain:** Board-approved operation/tolerance register, end-to-end maps, synthetic transaction traces, exercise result against each tolerance, reconciliation, remediation closure. **Source locator:** [APRA CPS 230, paras 27–47](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230).

**Regime: CPS 230 providers** **Responsible party and operative duty:** Entity: maintain material-provider policy/register, due diligence, concentration and fourth-party oversight, contracts and monitoring. Core technology is a presumptive material service. The 2026 limited contract exemptions do not remove underlying resilience duty. **Evidence a board or operator should retain:** Named functions, carrier/cloud/identity/payment providers and subcontractors, shared-provider analysis, failover capacity, contract rights and independent alarms. **Source locator:** [CPS 230, paras 48–60](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230); [APRA 2026 amendment notice](https://www.apra.gov.au/consultations/operational-risk-management).

**Regime: CPS 220 / CPS 190** **Responsible party and operative duty:** Board risk framework, appetite and limits; financial viability recovery/exit plans aligned with operational continuity. **Evidence a board or operator should retain:** Risk-appetite breaches, combined-risk analysis, liquidity links, recovery playbooks and consistency review. **Source locator:** [CPS 220, paras 21–29](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-220); [CPS 190, paras 1–4, 15 onward](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-190); [CPS 230 para. 18](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230).

**Regime: SOCI** **Responsible party and operative duty:** Responsible entity of a defined critical banking, telecommunications or data storage/processing asset: applicable register, risk-management-program and cyber-reporting duties depend on asset and entity status. Enhanced CIRMP categories commenced 10 June 2026 according to CISC guidance; check transition rule before assigning a deadline. **Evidence a board or operator should retain:** Asset classification, responsible-entity determination, program category, cyber significance assessment and notification clock. **Source locator:** [S02/F41 SOCI Act, ss. 12F–12G and Part 2B](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2018A00029/2026-06-04/2026-06-04/text/original/pdf); [S39/F41 CISC obligations, CIRMP and “Report cyber incidents”](https://www.cisc.gov.au/how-we-support-industry/regulatory-obligations).

**Regime: ACMA outage/customer rules** **Responsible party and operative duty:** Carrier/service provider: major-outage communications and register duties. Major threshold includes 100,000 services or all services on a state/territory network for over 60 minutes; significant local thresholds differ. Multi-channel updates at least every six hours in the first 24 hours. These duties do not guarantee bank data access. **Evidence a board or operator should retain:** Detection timestamp, affected service count/geography, channel messages, update log and register record. **Source locator:** [S37/F39 ACMA guidance, “Defining” and “Requirements”](https://www.acma.gov.au/communicating-customers-during-major-and-significant-local-outages-guidance-and-faqs-industry); [ACMA commencement notice](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2024-11/new-telco-industry-rules-major-outages).

**Regime: ACMA emergency calls** **Responsible party and operative duty:** Carrier and emergency-call participants: rules effective 1 Nov 2025 address network disconnection/camp-on, delivery testing, information exchange and reporting after major emergency-call outages. **Evidence a board or operator should retain:** End-to-end call-completion tests, alternate-network tests, incident exchange and corrective action. **Source locator:** [S38/F40 ACMA emergency-call FAQs, requirements](https://www.acma.gov.au/access-emergency-call-service-during-network-outages-faqs-industry).

## Notifications: classify every applicable trigger

**Trigger:** Critical operation disrupted **outside tolerance** **Who reports and clock:** APRA-regulated entity: **as soon as possible, within 24 hours**. **Practical distinction and source:** [CPS 230 para. 43](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230). Clock is tied to the outside-tolerance disruption, not merely BCP activation.

**Trigger:** Operational risk incident likely to have material financial impact or material impact on ability to maintain critical operations **Who reports and clock:** APRA-regulated entity: **within 72 hours** after awareness. **Practical distinction and source:** [CPS 230 para. 32](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230). Apply separately from the 24-hour threshold.

**Trigger:** Material agreement for a critical-operation service entered or materially changed **Who reports and clock:** APRA-regulated entity: **within 20 business days**. **Practical distinction and source:** [CPS 230 para. 60](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230). This is a provider-agreement notice, not an incident notice.

**Trigger:** Qualifying information-security incident / material control weakness unlikely to be promptly remediated **Who reports and clock:** APRA-regulated entity: **72 hours / 10 business days**, respectively. **Practical distinction and source:** [CPS 234 paras 35–36](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-234). [APRA notification guidance](https://www.apra.gov.au/about-us/our-functions/notify-breach-or-incident) says a single notice can cover overlapping CPS 230/234 events; earliest applicable deadline governs.

**Trigger:** Cyber incident with significant impact on availability / relevant impact **Who reports and clock:** SOCI responsible entity: **12 hours / 72 hours**, respectively. **Practical distinction and source:** [S39/F41 CISC, “Report cyber incidents”](https://www.cisc.gov.au/how-we-support-industry/regulatory-obligations). Cyber and asset-specific; not a general all-hazards outage clock.

**Trigger:** Major or significant local telco outage **Who reports and clock:** Covered carrier/service provider: customer notice and updates under ACMA standard; determine threshold and commencement before calculating. **Practical distinction and source:** [S37/F39 ACMA, “Defining” and “Requirements”](https://www.acma.gov.au/communicating-customers-during-major-and-significant-local-outages-guidance-and-faqs-industry). Customer communications differ from APRA/CISC regulator notifications.

**Trigger:** Major emergency-call outage **Who reports and clock:** Relevant telco/emergency-call participant: incident information exchange and reporting under applicable emergency-call rules. **Practical distinction and source:** [S38/F40 ACMA emergency-call FAQs](https://www.acma.gov.au/access-emergency-call-service-during-network-outages-faqs-industry). Check precise role and trigger.

**Correction.** A “10 business days after BCP activation” notice is sometimes assumed, but the current CPS 230 text does not support it. The 10-business-day clock cited above belongs to a **CPS 234 material control weakness**; CPS 230 also has a **20-business-day provider-agreement** notice. An event may engage several regimes and legal entities, so record trigger, awareness time, outside-tolerance time and responsible reporter separately.

## What incidents and official analysis mean for a bank

**Carrier and emergency calls — Fact and Judgement.** [S09–S10/F10–F12 Telstra’s commissioned review, pp. 2–10](https://www.telstra.com.au/content/dam/tcom/dynamic-media-projects/luke-campbell/TAPFindingsforTelstraOutage.pdf) identifies a timing-function failure, weak ownership/monitoring and eventual successful restoration. A bank should map the carrier functions behind branch links, ATM/EFTPOS connectivity where actually used, mobile authentication, contact centres and staff communications. Those bank dependencies are **institution-specific questions**, not documented impacts of the July Telstra outage. [S12/F13 ACMA’s 2023 Optus finding](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2024-11/optus-pays-12-million-penalty-triple-zero-outage) establishes a separate emergency-call failure and enforcement; [S13/F14 2025 Optus allegations](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-07/acma-takes-optus-mobile-court-over-september-2025-triple-zero-outage) were unresolved at cutoff. No rail cascade is asserted.

**Common providers and cloud — Fact and Judgement.** [S32 RBA FSR October 2026, §4.1](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/fsr/2026/oct/focus-topic-operational-risk-and-financial-stability.html) says some largest entities have around **150 providers supporting critical operations**, many serving multiple entities; cloud/storage concentration and limited substitutability can create a common exposure. It is incorrect to call these “150 shared providers.” [CISC S01/F02, pp. 10–13](https://www.cisc.gov.au/resources-subsite/Documents/critical-infrastructure-annual-risk-review-2026.pdf) adds cross-sector digital-supplier risk. No public bank-level overlap map or successful alternate-cloud capacity was established. **Judgement:** run degraded-service tests with the primary identity, DNS or cloud control plane unavailable; a contract or architecture diagram alone cannot show customer-level continuity.

**Payments and liquidity — Fact, counterevidence and Scenario.** [S33/F35 RBA’s 27 Jan 2026 incident report, pp. 2–7](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/rits/system-availability-statistics/2026/pdf/payments-settlement-outage-20260127.pdf) documents expired certificate disruption to file transfer, LVCS/LVSS and property settlement, plus monitoring, contact and duplicate-batch issues. **Counterevidence:** FSS settlement continued; [S35/F37 RBA statistics](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/rits/system-availability-statistics/2026/) show 100% RITS and FSS availability for September quarter 2026; [S36/F38 retail study](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2024/oct/the-reliability-of-retail-payment-services.html) shows high quarterly averages. **Scenario, not incident:** APRA’s system risk stress test imposed a three-working-day material-provider outage blocking debt-securities settlement during a liquidity shock; banks could use existing Exchange Settlement balances but could not monetise much other high-quality liquid assets ([RBA FSR §4.1, “System Risk Stress Test”](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/fsr/2026/oct/focus-topic-operational-risk-and-financial-stability.html); APRA System Risk Stress Test final report, 30 June 2026, S46, reports average bank Exchange Settlement balances about \$11 billion lower than baseline). Test payment backlog, collateral routes, counterparties and customer messaging together. Do not present the stress-test chain as an observed 2026 cascade.

## Minimum evidence pack for a joint exercise

Judgement derived from [CPS 230 paras 27–47](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230). Name each critical operation and its approved tolerances; record every primary and fallback carrier/cloud/identity/payment function and fourth party; inject a provider outage and independent alarm failure; capture timestamped login and payment transactions, settlement status, alternate-channel capacity, data loss, reconciliation and customer communications; compare actual outcomes with tolerances; preserve failed attempts and sign off retest. Cross-check the bank’s incident classification with the carrier’s ACMA obligations and any SOCI responsible entity’s cyber trigger. A shared exercise should measure *minimum service* under simultaneous demand, not just system uptime.

**Current-status limit.** Public records do not reveal a bank’s own tolerance levels or exercise results. CISC’s CIRMP categories and grace provisions require entity-specific rule checking. The Payments Crisis Coordination Framework is described as work in development by [RBA FSR §4.1, “Initiatives”](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/fsr/2026/oct/focus-topic-operational-risk-and-financial-stability.html); publication and exercise results were not established from the reviewed record. These gaps should remain open until primary evidence is obtained.
