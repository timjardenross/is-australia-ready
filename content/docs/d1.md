**Evidence cutoff:** 19 November 2026 · **Public-source assessment** · **Forward horizon:** November 2027–November 2029

## Decision in brief

**Judgement — medium confidence.** Australia has functioning crisis arrangements, sector regulation, incident learning and several concrete improvements. The reviewed public record does **not yet demonstrate** that interconnected essential services can continue or recover within specified limits during a prolonged, concurrent disruption. That is a conclusion about *public assurance*, not a finding that the system would fail. Supervisory, operator and classified evidence may be stronger. The most useful next step is to publish a bounded, independently evaluated cross-sector exercise with measured service outcomes and remediation ownership.

The distinction matters. The [Australian Government Crisis Management Framework (AGCMF)](https://www.pmc.gov.au/resources/australian-government-crisis-management-framework-agcmf/version-control) reached version **4.2 in September 2026**. [ANAO’s 2024 audit](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/australian-government-crisis-management-framework) called the framework largely appropriate, while finding no structured readiness assessment before the 2023 review and weaknesses in risk-based selection of national exercise scenarios (summary paras 9–12; paras 3.93–3.97). Those are dated audit findings; the 2026 revision demonstrates process change, not measured recovery under a multi-sector event. The framework chiefly covers near-term preparedness, response, relief and early recovery, rather than the full course of risk reduction and reconstruction ([AGCMF v4, pp. 1, 10](https://www.pmc.gov.au/sites/default/files/resource/download/agcmf-framework-v4.pdf)).

**D3 cross-reference:** AGCMF S16–S18/F18–F22; Telstra S09–S11/F10–F12; Optus S12–S13/F13–F14; RBA S32–S36/F33–F38; CISC S01/F01–F03; fuel S06/F08; electricity S05/F06; health S08/F09; AusAlert S14–S15/F15 and S48; APRA stress test S46. Direct source links and locators below control the claims.

## What the evidence establishes

1.  **Fact — shared functions can propagate disruption.** A [Telstra-commissioned external review](https://www.telstra.com.au/content/dam/tcom/dynamic-media-projects/luke-campbell/TAPFindingsforTelstraOutage.pdf) of 8 July 2026 traced a network-wide disturbance to timing-chassis work, an unupdated GPS card and earlier network design decisions (PDF pp. 2–4). Voice, messaging, data and some emergency calls were affected; most postpaid service returned by 12:12 pm (p. 4). It found weaknesses in ownership, change controls and monitoring (pp. 5–10). [Telstra accepted the findings](https://www.telstra.com.au/exchange/what-we-ve-learned-from-the-external-investigation-of-our-july-o). [ACMA opened an investigation](https://www.acma.gov.au/acma-statement-telstra-outage); the reviewed record does not establish a final breach determination. The review does **not** directly substantiate claims that this incident caused EFTPOS or regional-rail failures. Those edges are excluded from the observed cascade.

2.  **Fact — failure and recovery coexist.** [ACMA’s Optus enforcement record](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2024-11/optus-pays-12-million-penalty-triple-zero-outage) says 2,145 people could not access Triple Zero in the November 2023 outage and Optus subsidiaries paid over \$12 million in penalties. Separately, [ACMA’s July 2026 proceeding](https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-07/acma-takes-optus-mobile-court-over-september-2025-triple-zero-outage) alleges 1,005 failures relating to a September 2025 event; allegations are not adjudicated findings. Telstra’s published review says recovery worked well once the cause was isolated (pp. 4–5). Neither a successful restoration nor an enforcement action establishes sector-wide reliability.

3.  **Fact — concentration is real, its size is often misstated.** The [RBA’s October 2026 Financial Stability Review, §4.1](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/fsr/2026/oct/focus-topic-operational-risk-and-financial-stability.html) says *some of the largest regulated entities* have **around 150 service providers supporting critical operations**, with many providers serving multiple entities. It does not count 150 providers shared across the industry. The RBA highlights cloud/storage concentration, limited substitutability and opaque fourth/fifth parties. Its system risk stress test imposed a provider outage that blocked debt-securities settlement during liquidity stress; this was a **stress-test scenario**, not a historical national outage (§4.1, “System Risk Stress Test”).

4.  **Fact — measured outcomes are mixed.** An [RBA report on 27 January 2026](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/rits/system-availability-statistics/2026/pdf/payments-settlement-outage-20260127.pdf) documents an expired intermediate certificate affecting RITS file transfer, LVCS/LVSS and property settlement, while FSS settlement continued (pp. 2–6). [RBA availability statistics](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/rits/system-availability-statistics/2026/) show both a March-quarter RITS figure of 99.560% and September-quarter 100% for RITS and FSS. [RBA retail analysis](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2024/oct/the-reliability-of-retail-payment-services.html) found at least 99.8% average quarterly availability across services. Aggregate uptime therefore coexists with consequential individual failures.

5.  **Fact — national hazards have both exposure and adaptation.** [CISC’s 2026 annual review, pp. 4–17](https://www.cisc.gov.au/resources-subsite/Documents/critical-infrastructure-annual-risk-review-2026.pdf) describes shared suppliers, digital concentration and just-in-time logistics as systemic risks; its hospital and cable cascades are scenarios. The March 2026 fuel shock prompted a [PM&C Fuel Supply Taskforce](https://www.pmc.gov.au/domestic-policy/fuel-supply-taskforce) and [government fuel measures](https://www.energy.gov.au/news/securing-australias-fuel-supply), but these records do not establish that a national plan was first written during the crisis or that essential services failed nationwide. [AEMO’s 2026 ESOO](https://www.aemo.com.au/newsroom/media-release/2026-ESOO) forecasts **no NEM reliability gaps before 2030**, conditional on timely investment. [TGA’s May 2026 update](https://www.tga.gov.au/about-IV-fluids-shortages) says acute IV-fluid shortages had resolved. [NEMA reports AusAlert operational](https://www.nema.gov.au/about-us/media-centre/ausalert-now-operational), and the AusAlert site says it was ready for use from 1 October 2026 (the NEMA page is undated); its performance under simultaneous network failure is unproven.

## Assessment and decisions

**Assurance question:** Is national command defined? **Public status at cutoff: Fact: yes, substantially** **Evidence and limit:** AGCMF v4.2; [NEMA National Coordination Mechanism](https://www.nema.gov.au/ncm). Neither measures multi-sector recovery. **Practical decision:** Use existing arrangements for a joint exercise.

**Assurance question:** Are common dependencies mapped end to end? **Public status at cutoff: Judgement: partial public visibility** **Evidence and limit:** [CISC 2026, pp. 5, 10–13](https://www.cisc.gov.au/resources-subsite/Documents/critical-infrastructure-annual-risk-review-2026.pdf); [RBA FSR §4.1](https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/fsr/2026/oct/focus-topic-operational-risk-and-financial-stability.html). No public asset-level graph found in reviewed sources. **Practical decision:** Produce a releasable functional map, with sensitive detail held by operators/regulators.

**Assurance question:** Are service tolerances tested? **Public status at cutoff: Fact: duties exist; public results limited** **Evidence and limit:** [CPS 230, paras 34–47](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230) requires critical-operation tolerances and severe-but-plausible testing. Entity results are generally non-public. **Practical decision:** Require measured duration, minimum service and data loss in a joint exercise.

**Assurance question:** Are lessons independently closed? **Public status at cutoff: Judgement: uneven evidence** **Evidence and limit:** [ANAO 2024](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/australian-government-crisis-management-framework), [Telstra review pp. 5–10](https://www.telstra.com.au/content/dam/tcom/dynamic-media-projects/luke-campbell/TAPFindingsforTelstraOutage.pdf), [RBA RITS assessment §3.4](https://www.rba.gov.au/payments-and-infrastructure/rits/self-assessments/2026/pdf/2026-assessment-rits.pdf). Publication of actions is not proof of effectiveness. **Practical decision:** Track owners, deadlines, retests and an independent closure check.

**Assurance question:** Are supply chains uniformly brittle? **Public status at cutoff: Fact: no** **Evidence and limit:** [Productivity Commission 2021, executive summary](https://www.pc.gov.au/inquiries-and-research/supply-chains/report/) found most essential chains resilient; CISC identifies specific concentrations. **Practical decision:** Target single nodes and regional availability rather than blanket claims.

**Priority 1, next six months — Judgement.** Sponsor a scenario that removes one carrier function and one shared digital dependency while payment and emergency-call demand rises. Involve communications, payments, banking, emergency management and affected state operators. Publish a sanitised account of what continued, what fell below tolerance, when independent detection occurred and whether workarounds shared the failed dependency. This follows [CPS 230’s dependency and scenario requirements, paras 27–47](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-230) and the functional failure revealed by the [Telstra review](https://www.telstra.com.au/content/dam/tcom/dynamic-media-projects/luke-campbell/TAPFindingsforTelstraOutage.pdf).

**Priority 2, 2027 — Judgement.** Build a functional dependency baseline around named services: network timing and routing, emergency-call completion, cloud identity, payment messaging and settlement, fuel delivery, power at telecom sites and flood warning data. Record ownership, alternate routes, restoration time and known common suppliers. The [Northern Rivers flood study, pp. 65–66](https://www.nema.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-08/Characterisation%20of%20the%202022%20floods%20in%20the%20Northern%20Rivers%20region.pdf) and [NSW flood inquiry, para. 3.157](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/lcdocs/inquiries/2866/Report%20No%201%20-%20Response%20to%20major%20flooding%20across%20New%20South%20Wales%20in%202022.pdf) show why warning and communications nodes belong in the same map.

**Priority 3, 2027–29 — Judgement.** Report remediation in stages: rule or commitment; implemented control; exercised control; observed service outcome; independent validation. This avoids equating a framework update or funding award with resilience. [ANAO’s Disaster Ready Fund audit](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/award-of-funding-under-the-disaster-ready-fund) found awards largely effective but assessment only partly appropriate; it audited awards, not reduction in disaster losses (summary paras 9–18). [NEMA’s proposed recovery funding framework](https://www.nema.gov.au/our-work/disaster-recovery/disaster-recovery-funding-framework) remained in development in the [5 November 2026 ministers’ communiqué](https://www.nema.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-11/Final%20Communique%20-%20NEMMM%205%20November%202026.pdf).

## Forward view and limits

**Scenario — probability not estimated.** The most decision-useful stressors to 2029 are a repeat carrier fault affecting emergency calls; shared cloud or identity loss; disrupted fuel imports and local distribution; severe weather coinciding with local power and mobile outages; a provider or settlement outage during liquidity stress; and a changeover in emergency-call governance. These are tests, not forecasts. [AEMO’s no-gap NEM forecast](https://www.aemo.com.au/newsroom/media-release/2026-ESOO) is a counterweight to any claim of an expected near-term national electricity shortfall; it does not preclude local storm outages. [ASD’s 2025–26 activity counts](https://www.asd.gov.au/news/2026-10-27-australian-signals-directorate-releases-seventh-annual-cyber-threat-report-2025-26) describe reports and responses, not the probability of a coordinated attack.

**Evidence boundary.** No public national recovery-time distribution, complete shared-provider map or independently audited multi-sector exercise result was identified in the public sources reviewed. That search limit does not prove nonexistence. The July 2026 Telstra-to-EFTPOS and Telstra-to-rail claims, a claim that the fuel plan was written during the shock, and qualitative high/moderate/low scenario probabilities are omitted because the reviewed primary sources do not substantiate them. D4 contains the edge-level evidence; D5 defines the watch; D6 sets out the finance and telecommunications duties.
